## One, the policy change

### Every building gets eleven characters, and you should check yours

Nigeria switched on a national digital postcode system on 1 October. The Federal Government is running it through the Nigerian Postal Service, NIPOST, and Dr Bosun Tijani, Minister of Communications, Innovation and Digital Economy, announced the rollout. The Federal Executive Council approved it in March.

Every addressable building gets an eleven-character alphanumeric code, in the shape FC 02 A09 DB 09. The first two letters carry the state or the Federal Capital Territory, and the segments after that narrow down through local government area, postal district, zone and finally the individual building. It is assigned to the structure rather than to a person, and it replaces the old six-digit numeric codes. You can look yours up at postcode.gov.ng, or through the mobile app. The geospatial partner Milsat built the lookup interface with NIPOST.

Two things are worth knowing before you assume it works. The first is cost. Looking up, saving and sharing your own code is free. Beyond that it is tiered, and only basic search and the shallowest lookup are free without an API key. The levels above that need corporate verification and purchased credits, and the level that returns exact coordinates needs special regulatory approval. The second is coverage. Neither the ministry nor NIPOST has published a count of how many structures are actually mapped, and first-day reporting from Nairametrics on 2 October found duplicate codes on neighbouring buildings, real residences missing entirely, and thin coverage outside the cities. Whether the old six-digit codes still work, and for how long, has not been stated either way.

<p class="why"><b>Why it matters to you.</b> There is a small job here worth doing this week, and it takes two minutes. Look up your own building, your office and anywhere you take deliveries, and check that what comes back is actually right. A wrong or duplicated code is far easier to get corrected now, while the system is new and somebody is still watching the error reports, than it will be in a year when banks are checking addresses against it and a courier treats it as settled fact. If you sell and deliver anything, the figure being used to justify the whole project is what Businessday puts at 80 billion naira a year lost to failed deliveries, which tells you how seriously the commercial integrations are going to be pushed. The question nobody has answered is the one worth holding on to. A permanent code attached to a building, with federal and state systems being lined up to read it, is a quietly powerful piece of infrastructure, and the privacy terms around who gets to query it have had far less attention than the launch did.</p>

## Two, the funding move

### Ten deals took 85 percent of the month

Figures here are from the Nairametrics Research Team, published on 8 October and covering disclosed deals in September 2026. They count differently from the cumulative trackers used in Brief #01 and Brief #03, so read them as a snapshot of one month rather than a running total that lines up with either of those.

African startups raised 260.3 million dollars across 54 disclosed deals in September. The top ten of those deals accounted for 222.6 million, or 85.5 percent of everything raised. That is down 40.2 percent from August, which brought in 435.2 million, and up 88.2 percent on September 2025, which brought in 138.3 million. The deal count fell over the year, from 62 to 54.

The country split is the part to sit with. Egypt took 74.6 million across nine deals. Kenya took 54.2 million, also across nine. Nigeria took 11.3 million, likewise across nine deals, which is 4.34 percent of the month's total. Debt made up 112.1 million of the overall figure, 43.1 percent, from twelve deals. Seed rounds accounted for 6.4 million across four deals, and pre-seed for 2.7 million across three.

<p class="why"><b>Why it matters to you.</b> Nine Nigerian deals shared 11.3 million dollars in a month when nine Egyptian deals shared 74.6 million. Same number of deals, roughly a seventh of the money. If you are raising here, that ratio is a better planning input than any continental headline, because it describes cheque size rather than appetite. Look at the bottom of the table too. Seed and pre-seed together came to 9.1 million dollars across seven deals on the whole continent in a month. Early-stage capital is the thinnest layer in the stack right now, and the thing that follows from that is unglamorous but true. Build to revenue earlier than you planned to, and treat a raise as something that accelerates a business that already works rather than something that starts one.</p>

## Three, the free course

### A free Microsoft credential, and an honest word about its length

The AI Skills 4 Women programme is run by Founderz with Microsoft, and is being promoted to Nigerian women through a dedicated Federal Ministry of Women Affairs page. It is open to women, it is free, and it is entirely online.

It covers AI fundamentals, practical applications, prompt engineering and ethics, AI cybersecurity and data analytics, and a leadership and mentorship element. Finish it and you get an official Microsoft credential you can put on your LinkedIn profile, plus access to a community of women working in AI. No closing date has been published, and registration was open when we checked on 8 October.

Now the part a listing will not tell you. The course runs to about three hours. That is the real figure, and it changes what this is. Three hours is not a retraining programme and nobody should plan a career move around it.

<p class="why"><b>Why it matters to you.</b> Judge it as what it is and it is worth the afternoon. A credential issued by Microsoft, at no cost, in an afternoon, is a reasonable trade, particularly if your profile currently says nothing at all about AI and you have been meaning to fix that. Do not oversell it on your CV and do not let it stand in for depth. The more interesting thing is behind it. The 2025 edition of this programme awarded 150 scholarships to a separate, paid AI and Innovation Certificate Program, and the free course is the doorway people walked through to get them. If that is the actual prize, then the three hours are not the training. They are the application.</p>

## Four, the tool

### A professional design suite that stopped charging

Affinity is the design suite built by Serif, which Canva bought in March 2024 for around 300 million pounds. On 30 October 2025 Canva released version 3, merged what used to be three separate applications, Designer, Photo and Publisher, into a single app, and made it free.

One app now holds three workspaces. Vector, which was Designer. Pixel, which was Photo. Layout, which was Publisher. You move between them without leaving the document, and you can build your own arrangement. It runs on macOS and Windows, with an iPad version listed as coming soon. The core application costs nothing, with no trial window and no feature held back behind a licence, and Canva states plainly that Affinity is free for individuals.

There is one boundary to know about. Canva's AI features inside it, background removal, Expand and Edit, and generative fill, run on a Canva Pro subscription. Everything that was in the paid Affinity suite before is in the free app. Version 2 still runs for anybody who bought it, but it is no longer actively maintained.

<p class="why"><b>Why it matters to you.</b> We wrote a few weeks ago about how much of a Nigerian digital professional's tool stack is priced in dollars while the work is paid for in naira. This is the rare move in the other direction, and it is worth acting on deliberately rather than just noting. If you are paying a monthly subscription for vector or raster work, spend an evening rebuilding one real piece of recent work in Affinity before your next renewal date. Not a tutorial file, something you were actually paid for, because that is the only test that tells you whether the switch holds up under your own habits. If it does, you have removed a recurring dollar cost from your business. If it does not, you have lost an evening and learned something concrete.</p>

## Five, the report worth reading

### The people who hire now rank portfolios above degrees

The Linux Foundation, through LF Research and LF Education, published the 2026 State of Tech Talent report in May, under the subtitle Not a jobs crisis, but a skills crisis with an upskilling answer.

Read the methodology first, because it decides how much weight to give any of it. The survey was fielded in February 2026. Of 1,077 people who started it, 530 finished and 400 valid responses were analysed. Every respondent had to be employed and personally responsible for hiring, recruiting or training IT professionals, which is a genuinely useful filter. The margin of error is 4.1 percent at 90 percent confidence. And the caveat that matters most for anyone reading this from Lagos or Abuja, Africa made up about 3 percent of respondents and the report offers no Africa-specific findings at all. It is a window onto how employers in Europe, Asia-Pacific and North America are thinking, not a description of the Nigerian market.

On that basis, three findings travel. Upskilling existing staff is now the leading answer to a skills gap, at 57 percent, ahead of hiring new people at 49 percent, and organisations reported being 3.5 times more likely to upskill than to hire, up from 3.1 times a year earlier. The reason sits in the next figure. New hires are taking 53 percent longer to reach productivity, and 28 percent of them leave within six months, up from 19 percent. Then the ranking. Asked what matters in assessing a candidate, 93 percent of hiring managers said hands-on experience and 87 percent said project portfolios, against 76 percent for skill certifications and 74 percent for formal degrees.

<p class="why"><b>Why it matters to you.</b> That last ranking is the sentence to act on, and it inverts the order most people were taught. A portfolio of real work outranks a certificate, and a certificate outranks a degree, in the stated preferences of the people doing the hiring. Put next to item three in this Brief, it argues for a specific sequence. Take the free credential, because it costs an afternoon. Then spend the next month turning it into two or three pieces of finished, documented work, because that is the thing being ranked eleven points higher. The other half of the finding is for anyone who runs a team. If hiring is slower and more fragile than it used to be, the cheapest capacity available to you is the people already on your payroll, and almost nobody budgets for that properly.</p>
