## One, the policy change

### The registry has a date, and it is Tuesday

Brief #02 covered the Nigerian Communications Commission's Device Management System, the central registry that ties every mobile phone sold or activated in Nigeria to its IMEI number. The part that was missing then is now fixed. Existing device stock has to be registered by Tuesday 6 October. The original date was 7 September, and the extension to 6 October was communicated to importers by email rather than announced.

At the NCC's stakeholder session, importers and original equipment manufacturers said the extra month is not enough for businesses that have not started. Three other concerns came up and none of them has been answered. There is no defined appeal process for a device flagged because its IMEI number is legitimately damaged. Stakeholders questioned whether the platform can handle the volume of devices expected to pass through it, and during live demonstrations they ran into difficulties, which the NCC attributed to system upgrades in progress. The Commission has also not said what the technician training element will cost the people required to do it.

<p class="why"><b>Why it matters to you.</b> If you import, sell or repair phones, Tuesday is the live obligation and the registration of existing stock is the thing to get done. If you do not, the detail to watch is the missing appeal route. A registry that can flag a handset without a published way to un-flag it moves the cost of its own mistakes onto whoever is holding the phone. That is a design choice, not an oversight, and it is still fixable before Tuesday.</p>

## Two, the funding move

### Two billion dollars, and why that reads worse than it looks

African startups crossed two billion dollars raised in 2026 on 30 September, according to Africa: The Big Deal. It is a round number and it arrived a month later than the same milestone in 2025, though three months earlier than in 2024, which did not cross the line until December.

The figures underneath are the ones to read. From January to July, African startups raised 1.46 billion dollars, down 27 percent on the same period in 2025. Equity funding fell 9 percent year on year to 921 million dollars. Debt funding fell 44 percent, to 529 million dollars. If the current rate holds, the year could finish near three billion.

<p class="why"><b>Why it matters to you.</b> A milestone is a flattering way to report a decline. The headline number went up because the calendar moved, and the like for like comparison went down. The detail worth your attention is the split. A 9 percent fall in equity is an investor mood. A 44 percent fall in debt is a lending retreat, and debt is what funds working capital in businesses that hold stock or equipment. If you are raising into this, plan for a longer process and a smaller cheque, and do not read a continental total as a signal about your own round.</p>

## Three, the free course

### Twelve funded weeks, if your address is in Enugu

The limit first, because it decides whether the rest is relevant to you. The Tech4Youth Advanced ICT Skills Training Programme is open only to people resident in Enugu State, aged 18 to 35. If that is not you, skip to item four.

It is twelve weeks of fully funded ICT training in one of four tracks, data analytics, cybersecurity, software development or artificial intelligence. It is implemented by TechQuest STEM Academy under the GIZ Digital Transformation Centre Nigeria, with joint funding from the German Federal Ministry for Economic Cooperation and Development and the Danish Ministry of Foreign Affairs. No prior technology experience is required. Women and persons with disabilities are particularly encouraged to apply. Applications run on a rolling basis while places remain, with no published closing date.

<p class="why"><b>Why it matters to you.</b> What makes this one worth the application is not the twelve weeks. It is what is wrapped around them, mentorship, CV and LinkedIn work, portfolio building, interview preparation and employment support. Free technical training is common and the follow-through almost never is, because teaching is cheap to fund and placement is not. A programme that budgets for the second half is making a harder promise than most. If you are in Enugu and have been self-teaching without anything to show for it, this is the gap it fills.</p>

## Four, the tool

### A paid AI plan, free for a year, on a student email

Google is giving Nigerian students twelve months of Google AI Plus at no cost. The plan normally costs 92,400 naira a year. It includes Gemini with its Guided Learning mode, Deep Research, Gemini Live for voice conversations, study notebooks, custom practice quizzes, unlimited file uploads, step-by-step problem solving through Google Lens, and 400GB of cloud storage.

Eligibility is being 18 or over and currently enrolled at an accredited Nigerian university, polytechnic or college of education. Enrolment is verified through SheerID, so you will need proof of current registration. Claim it at the Gemini student offer page. The offer opened in August and no closing date has been published, which is a reason to do it now rather than later.

<p class="why"><b>Why it matters to you.</b> A free year of a paid tier is long enough to find out whether a tool changes how you work, and that is the only question worth asking of it. Use the year deliberately. Pick two or three things you actually do, reading long documents, drafting, working through problem sets, and see whether it makes them faster or just noisier. Then you will know in twelve months whether 92,400 naira is worth it to you, which is a far better position than guessing now. Also worth saying plainly, verification runs on your enrolment status, so this is a window that closes when you graduate.</p>

## Five, the report worth reading

### What digital skills are worth, in percentage terms

GSMA Intelligence published Bridging the Divide, Enhancing Digital Literacy in the AI Era, in September, launched at UNESCO's Digital Learning Week. Worth knowing before you read it, the research was produced in partnership with Huawei, which sells handsets and network equipment and runs its own digital skills programmes. That does not make the numbers wrong. It does mean the recommendations point somewhere convenient.

The framing figure is this. Mobile broadband now covers almost 96 percent of the world's population, and 3.1 billion people who live inside that coverage still do not use mobile internet. More than one in three people, 38 percent, are offline while sitting under a signal. The report estimates that closing the digital capability gap could add around 3.5 trillion dollars to global GDP by 2030, with more than 90 percent of that falling to low and middle income countries.

The finding that should travel is narrower and more useful. Generative AI literacy carries a wage premium of up to 36 percent, higher than the return on standard AI skills and higher than the return on traditional digital skills. The full report runs to 42 pages and sits behind a short registration form.

<p class="why"><b>Why it matters to you.</b> Hold on to the 36 percent. Almost every argument for learning a new tool is made on vague employability, which is unfalsifiable and therefore easy to ignore. This one puts a number on it, and it is specific about where the premium sits, in generative AI literacy rather than digital skills in general. Then read it next to item three. The wage premium is national. The funded training that unlocks it is open to a few thousand people in one state. That distance is the actual problem, and it is most of the reason we keep hunting down the free courses in this Brief.</p>
